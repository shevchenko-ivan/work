Miele Ukraine — a UX audit before the redesign.

An expert review of shop.miele.ua ahead of a planned migration and redesign of the B2C shop. Four key surfaces, 37 findings, each with its impact on the buyer and a concrete recommendation — delivered as a document the client could act on directly.

The business problem: where does a premium shop lose buyers?

Miele Ukraine sells premium home appliances to buyers who already trust the brand and expect the online shop to match it. The company was planning a migration and redesign of its B2C shop and wanted an outside view first: what exactly hurts conversion and trust, and what should be fixed first.

In the brief the client named the two funnels with the highest drop-off — the product page and checkout. That set the priorities of the audit before I opened the site.

How I scoped and ran it.

  1. A short brief and a kickoff callBusiness goals, audience, competitors and problem funnels were fixed in a brief. The scope was agreed as a fixed-price package covering four surfaces.
  2. Scenario walkthroughsThe key buyer scenarios — viewing a product, the cart, checkout — checked against usability heuristics and e-commerce conventions.
  3. Competitor patternsProduct page structure, calls to action and information blocks compared with Bosch, Siemens and Gaggenau.
  4. Technical and accessibility checksPage speed for the home and product pages, responsive behaviour, and colour contrast against WCAG AA. One example: the price text measured 3.59 : 1.
  5. One format for every findingA screenshot with the problem marked, then three lines: the problem, its impact, the recommendation. 47 annotated screenshots in total.

Four surfaces, ranked by impact.

  • Checkout — critical, 11 findingsFour separate steps, errors shown before the buyer types anything and away from the fields, the name asked twice, an account created without a clear notice.
  • Home, sign-in and registration — high, 17 findingsThe catalogue hidden in a burger menu, three pop-up widgets at once, and banners that speak only about discounts with no premium positioning.
  • Search — high, 4 findingsA narrow search field that overlaps the header icons, a search icon that is easy to miss, unclear pagination.
  • Product page — medium, 6 findingsThree competing purchase buttons, a barely visible old price, and hidden strengths such as the 3D view and the warranty.

A roadmap the client could act on.

  1. Rebuild checkout end to endFewer steps, inline validation, no repeated fields.
  2. Bring the catalogue to the first screenA category block on the home page, so a buyer finds their appliance type in seconds.
  3. Make search visibleA wider field and a clear icon first; autocomplete and filters as the next stage.
  4. One primary action on the product pageA single main button, with supporting actions de-emphasised.
  5. Test the critical changesMeasure the effect on conversion before extending the redesign.

Outcome — and its limits.

The client received a document to navigate: findings by surface, priorities, and a starting point for whoever builds the redesign. This was an expert review, not a study with users — so the report recommends testing the new checkout with real buyers as the next step.
