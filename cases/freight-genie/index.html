Freight Genie — a sales CRM for freight companies.

An MVP of a multi-role SaaS CRM where freight sales teams move shippers from a first call to a recurring customer. Three user roles, a lead pipeline, tasks, documents and permissions — designed solo as a fixed-price Upwork project that earned a five-star review.

Year
2020
Client
Upwork (confidential)

Scope
MVP · 3 roles

The business problem: a sales process that lived in people’s heads.

The client was building a product for freight companies. Their sales people win shippers through a long chain of calls, quotes and first shipments. Without a system, nobody could see which leads were moving and which had gone quiet.

The client came with a written specification for an MVP and a fixed budget. My job was to turn that specification into a complete, buildable design — without the scope growing along the way.

Three roles, three views of the same data.

  • AdminThe product’s own staff: manages customer companies and can see their dashboards.
  • Customer OwnerRuns a freight company’s account: leads, company settings, sales users and analytics.
  • Customer SalesWorks the leads assigned to them, day by day.

I started from this permission map and not from screens. For every role I wrote down what they must see, what they may change and where they enter the product. The screens followed from that.

How I worked inside a fixed price.

  1. Specification to flowsI turned the client’s document into a mind map of flows and confirmed it with the client before opening Figma.
  2. A shared boardEvery screen was a card on a board the client could see, with a clear done state. Nothing moved forward on assumptions.
  3. States, not only screensEmpty, first-use and error states were designed together with the main flow, so developers did not have to invent them.

What I designed.

  • Pipeline dashboardLeads counted by stage — from first contact to recurring customer or closed-lost — so an owner sees the health of sales at a glance.
  • Lead pageOne place for a shipper’s details, the history of contact and notes.
  • Lead pool and assignmentA shared pool of leads and a simple way to assign a lead to a sales person.
  • TasksA task creation dialog and a list of all tasks sorted by date.
  • Deals and documentsDeal screens, and documents organised in folders.
  • Roles and permissionsA settings screen for roles, adding employees, and sign-in and sign-up flows.

What I carry into multi-role products today.

  • Permissions firstIn a multi-role product, the permission map is the real information architecture. Draw it before any screen.
  • A pipeline is a state machineEvery screen in a CRM is a place where a status changes. Designing the statuses first makes the screens obvious.
  • Fixed price needs a visible scopeA shared board with a done state protects both sides better than any contract clause.
